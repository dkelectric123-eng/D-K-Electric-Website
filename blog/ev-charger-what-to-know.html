What to Know Before Installing an EV Charger at Home

Level 1 and Level 2 are not the same job

Level 1 is the cord that came with the car plugged into an ordinary outlet. It needs no electrician and it adds range slowly — fine if the car sits overnight and the commute is short.

Level 2 runs on 240 volts, the same as a dryer or a range, and charges several times faster. It needs its own dedicated circuit. That is the job people mean when they say they are getting a charger installed, and it is not something to hang off an existing outlet.

The panel gets checked before anything is ordered

A Level 2 charger adds a large continuous load. Whether a house can take one depends on two separate things: whether the service has the electrical capacity, and whether the panel has the physical space for another double-pole breaker. A panel can pass on one and fail on the other.

This check comes first because the answer sometimes changes the job — from a straightforward install into a panel upgrade. Finding that out after the charger has been bought and a date booked helps nobody.

The permit is not optional, and it protects you

A charger is a new dedicated circuit, and that is permitted, inspected work. Skipping it creates two problems that show up later: an insurer can ask questions after any incident on that circuit, and unpermitted electrical work complicates a house sale when it surfaces on an inspection.

What decides how big the job is

  • Distance from the panel to the charger. The single biggest factor. A garage on the other side of the panel wall is a different job to a detached garage across a driveway.
  • What the route looks like. An unfinished basement or open garage ceiling is straightforward. Finished walls, brick, or crossing an outside space is not.
  • Indoors or out. An outdoor run needs conduit and enclosures rated for it.
  • Panel capacity. If the service cannot carry the load, the panel becomes part of the job.

Buying the charger yourself

That is fine, and common. The requirement is that it is a listed product suitable for where it is going — indoor or outdoor rated as appropriate. Brand matters much less than people expect: the wiring, the circuit and the installation are the same work whether the badge says Tesla, Ford, GM or Rivian.
