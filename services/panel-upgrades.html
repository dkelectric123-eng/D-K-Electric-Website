What this actually involves

Houses ask more of their panels than they used to. Air conditioning, a second fridge, a hot tub, a car charger and a finished basement all land on a service that may have been sized decades ago. Questions

Straight answers

How do I know if my panel needs upgrading?

Common signs are a panel with no spare breaker spaces, breakers that trip as normal household load goes on, a service rated below 100 amps, or a panel brand your home inspector or insurer flagged. Adding a large new load like an EV charger or central air is also a natural moment to check.

Do you handle the ComEd side?

Yes. A service upgrade needs the utility to disconnect and reconnect, and we coordinate that as part of the job so it happens on schedule.

Will my power be off all day?

There is an outage while the panel is swapped — that part is unavoidable. How long depends on the scope. We tell you what to expect before the day so you can plan around it.

Is a permit required for a panel upgrade?

Yes, and it protects you. Permitted and inspected work is what you want on record when you sell the house or make an insurance claim.
